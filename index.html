01 — The offer

The 10-Day Compliance Sprint

A buyer sends a security questionnaire. You have no answers. The deal goes quiet, then it dies. That is what missing paperwork actually costs, and it is the thing this fixes.

What lands in your inbox on day ten

  1. 01

    The full policy set

    Fourteen policies mapped to SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, ISO 27001 Annex A and NIST CSF. Written so a new hire understands them on the first read.

  2. 02

    Risk assessment and register

    Your risks, not a generic list. Scored, with a named owner and a review date against each one.

  3. 03

    Incident response plan

    Plus a one-page runbook for the three things that actually happen: phishing, ransomware, and someone leaving a laptop in a taxi.

  4. 04

    Security questionnaire answer bank

    Pre-written answers to the questions enterprise buyers keep sending. This is the piece that gets stalled deals moving again.

  5. 05

    Gap analysis and roadmap

    What you are missing, ordered by what costs you a deal soonest. Split into fix today, fix this month, fix this quarter.

  6. 06

    Staff awareness pack

    Short, plain-language training your team will finish. In English, Nigerian Pidgin or Yoruba, depending on who has to read it.

  7. 07

    Vendor review template

    So you can run the same assessment on your own suppliers instead of paying someone again next quarter.

  8. 08

    Thirty days of support after delivery

    You send questions, I answer them. No hourly meter running while you read your own documents.

The guarantee

Delivered in 10 business days, or you don't pay.

And if your auditor or your client pushes back on anything I wrote, I revise it free until it clears. You are not buying a stack of files. You are buying the part where it holds up.

Worth your time if

You are a 5 to 200 person B2B company, and customers have started asking for SOC 2 or ISO 27001 before they'll sign.

Not worth your time if

03 — About

Why I only write for small companies.

I started out writing Python scripts in college. Then I sat down one afternoon and read the blogs of five major security vendors back to back. CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Fortinet, Thales, SentinelOne. Every single one was written for a CISO who already has a team and a budget.

The companies getting hit are the ones with neither. A twenty-person agency. A clinic holding patient records with no IT person. They open that content, bounce off the acronyms, and go back to work unprotected, because nobody ever told them what they needed to hear in words they use.

So that is the job. I write the security paperwork small companies need, build tools that automate the boring parts, and explain all of it in plain English. Right now I am a cybersecurity intern at Samafi Cyberhub, I hold the Google Cybersecurity and Oracle Cloud certificates, and I tutor students in security and AI for free because somebody did that for me.

06 — Questions

The things people ask before booking.

Short answers. If yours isn't here, email me and I'll add it.

How can you write in 10 days what consultants take six months to do?

Consulting firms bill by the hour, so the calendar fills up with meetings. Writing the documents was never the slow part. I run one 45-minute scoping call, write from your real stack, send drafts on day five and the final set on day ten.

Are these just templates with my logo on them?

No. A template is the skeleton. Your risk register, gap analysis and questionnaire answers come out of your actual tools, vendors and workflows. Generic documents don't survive an auditor or a serious buyer, which would defeat the point of buying them.

Will this make my company SOC 2 certified?

No, and be careful with anyone who promises it will. Certification needs an accredited auditor and an observation window. What you get is every document the audit asks for, plus the roadmap to close your gaps. Documentation is where most companies stall, and that part is handled.

What do you need from me during the sprint?

One 45-minute call, a list of the tools and vendors you use, and one person who can answer questions by message. That's the whole ask.

Do you work with companies outside Nigeria?

Yes, the work is remote. I'm in Lagos and I work across West African, European and US time zones.

What does it cost?

It depends on your headcount and how many frameworks you need. It costs less than one month of a consulting retainer. Tell me your size on the call and you get a number that day, not a proposal deck two weeks later.

Can you do the technical security work too, or only the paperwork?

Both. I build intrusion detection tooling, test access controls, and run AI red teaming against LLM applications. Several of those tools are public on my GitHub if you want to read the code before you trust the claim.
